Enterprise Forms and Workflow Plan

⬇ Download PDF Word

Prepared for Yoseph Demissie, Chief Information Officer · Prepared by Eric Crutchfield · October 27, 2026 · Proposed approach for discussion

Purpose and direction

Establish a reusable enterprise framework for internal forms, secure external file submissions, and request tracking, using SharePoint as the proposed foundation with supporting Microsoft 365 tools. Begin with IT as the pilot, then extend the framework to Human Resources, Institutional Effectiveness, Academic Affairs, and other departments.

This plan sets direction only. Detailed requirements, costs, responsibilities, and schedules will be developed during planning.

HOW TO READ THIS PLAN

Required items are agreed outcomes and controls the solution must meet. Proposed items are tools and configurations to be selected during detailed design and validated through the pilots. Nothing in this plan commits to a product, license, budget, or date.

Requirements

REQUIREMENT 1

Internal forms portal

Create a central SharePoint entry point where employees can find, access, complete, and submit departmental forms. Begin by inventorying IT’s existing shared folder of fillable PDFs and organizing approved forms by department and purpose.

Retain PDFs where appropriate and identify opportunities for online forms that support routing and structured tracking. Confirm how each form will be completed and submitted before migration.

EXPECTED RESULT

  • A consistent employee experience.
  • A departmental structure that can expand without rebuilding the solution.

REQUIREMENT 2

Secure external submissions

Develop a controlled process for receiving documents, images, videos, and other files from vendors and external parties. Distinguish general secure file collection from controlled RFP submissions. Both require confidential handling and access limited to authorized staff; RFPs require additional controls.

REQUIRED RFP CONTROLS

  • Verified vendor identity.
  • Enforced submission deadlines.
  • Timestamped receipts confirming successful submission.
  • Restricted access to bid contents until the authorized opening.
  • Vendors cannot view other submissions.

A basic upload link alone does not meet these requirements. General file collection may use a simpler process where appropriate.

REQUIREMENT 3

Request lifecycle and auditability

Capture the full history of a request, from submission through review, approval or denial, fulfillment, and closure. Initial examples include acceptable use policy acknowledgments or related approvals, and onboarding requests.

Record supporting documents, responsible staff, decision details, dates, and actions taken so each request can be traced and reviewed.

EXPECTED RESULT

  • A durable business record that supports status reporting, accountability, and audits.

Implementation approach

The work proceeds in four phases. Durations and resourcing will be set in the detailed implementation plan.

Phase Key activities

PHASE 1

Confirm requirements and standards
  • Confirm priority use cases with Yoseph and departmental owners.
  • Inventory IT forms and select one representative request workflow.
  • Set common standards for organization, naming, accessibility, ownership, permissions, retention, and reporting.
  • Review Microsoft 365 licensing and external sharing settings.

PHASE 2

Build and evaluate the IT pilot
  • Create the enterprise structure and the IT forms area.
  • Migrate the initial approved forms.
  • Implement one request process with routing, decision capture, status tracking, and closure.
  • Test with a small employee group and refine before broader use.

PHASE 3

Validate external intake
  • Pilot general secure file collection and a controlled RFP scenario with the responsible department.
  • Test vendor verification, deadline enforcement and late-submission handling, timestamped receipts, and attempts to access bids before opening or to view another vendor’s files.
  • Confirm file limits, purchasing requirements, and authorized opening procedures.
  • Resolve control gaps before any live solicitation.

PHASE 4

Expand by department
  • Apply the tested framework to HR and other departments based on business priorities.
  • Assign departmental content and process owners and provide brief training.
  • Establish ongoing support and review procedures.

Supporting tools and platform

The components below are proposed roles across internal forms, external submissions, and request tracking. Final products, licensing, and configuration will be selected during design and validated through the pilots.

Component Proposed role To validate
SharePoint Departmental portal, document libraries, controlled file storage, and structured request and submission records in SharePoint Lists. External intake kept separate from internal working areas. Site structure, permissions model, and separation of external intake.
Power Automate Routing, notifications, submission receipts, approvals, and recording of process events. Its role in deadline and opening procedures; enforcement across every submission and access path.
Forms and application interfaces Structured data entry and guided submissions: Microsoft Forms or Power Apps for suitable internal processes; an authenticated external interface such as Power Pages where vendor requirements warrant it. External file upload support and licensing.
Identity and access controls Microsoft Entra external identity capabilities and application permissions to verify vendors and limit access. That bid contents stay inaccessible to reviewers until the authorized opening, with privileged access governed and audited.
Audit and retention Microsoft Purview and platform logging and retention to preserve records and activity history. That decisions, timestamps, receipts, and opening events are stored as durable records with retention aligned to institutional requirements.

TECHNICAL VALIDATION

SharePoint is the proposed foundation. Do not assume SharePoint and Power Automate alone satisfy the complete RFP process. Validate identity assurance, deadline enforcement, receipt delivery, and protection of bid contents, including from administrators.

If Microsoft 365 components cannot meet the required controls, evaluate a dedicated procurement submission platform and its integration with the enterprise framework.

Governance and ownership

  • Assign departmental content and process owners and technical support responsibilities.
  • Establish access, retention, and audit requirements before launch.
  • Preserve decision history in the business record; configure technical audit logging separately.
  • Confirm licensing, configuration, and operating responsibilities during design.

Measures of success

Area Success looks like
Internal forms Employees can locate and submit the correct forms.
External submissions External parties can submit files without seeing other submissions.
RFP pilot Demonstrates verified identity, enforced deadlines, successful receipt delivery, and restricted bid access until the authorized opening.
Request tracking Each pilot request can be traced through closure.
Expansion Departmental owners confirm readiness before their area goes live.

Immediate next steps

  1. Confirm this overall direction with Yoseph.
  2. Identify the IT forms and the initial workflow for the pilot.
  3. Designate business owners for the three work areas.

These decisions will shape the detailed implementation plan and schedule.

Portal Design and Build Guide

⬇ Download PDF Word

Requirement 1, internal forms portal, IT pilot · Prepared by Eric Crutchfield · October 27, 2026 · Proposed design for team review

Purpose

This guide describes the proposed design of the WCCCD Forms Center and how to build it in SharePoint Online for the IT pilot. It covers site structure, the forms library, page layouts, search, content lifecycle, and known limitations. Secure external submissions (Requirement 2) and request tracking (Requirement 3) are outside its scope.

DESIGN PRINCIPLES

  1. One library, many views. Every form lives in one document library. Pages show filtered views of it. Adding a department means adding a page and tagging forms.
  2. Standard SharePoint first. The pilot uses out-of-the-box sites, web parts, views, and column formatting. Add-ons are considered only when usage shows a need.
  3. Every form row answers "how do I submit this?" Format, submission steps, owner, and last review date travel with the form.
  4. Show only what works. Features and departments appear when they are live.
  5. Accessible by default. Pages and published forms meet WCAG 2.1 AA.

Site structure

Figure 1. Site structure and employee navigation flow. Grey dashed items are later phases.

Home Entry point: task shortcuts, department cards, help. Quick Links, Text, Image, Button

Site navigation

Home | All forms | Departments (menu: Information Technology) | Help. Cascading menu style is enough at this size. Add each department to the menu when its forms are published. Add My requests when request tracking goes live.

Forms library

One document library, Forms. Fillable PDFs are uploaded as files. Online forms (Microsoft Forms or Power Apps) are added as link items pointing to the form. Turn on content approval and major/minor versioning. Set draft item security so only authors and approvers see unapproved items.

Columns

Column Type Values and notes Shown to employees
Title Built-in Plain task name, e.g. "Equipment Request". No version numbers. Yes (link)
Form ID Single line Department prefix and number, e.g. IT-001. Never reused. Yes
Description Multiple lines, plain One sentence on what the form is for. Yes
Department Choice Information Technology; later HR, Institutional Effectiveness, Academic Affairs. Filter
Category Choice IT: Accounts and Access, Equipment, Software, Policies and Acknowledgments. Group
Task Choice, multi-select Get access to a system; Request equipment or software; Starting or leaving a job; Policies and acknowledgments. Filter
Format Choice Fillable PDF; Online form. Shown as a badge via column formatting. Yes
How to submit Multiple lines, plain Where and how to send it, e.g. "Get supervisor signature, then email to the IT Help Desk." Yes
Content owner Person Named person responsible for accuracy. No
Last reviewed Date Set at each review. Displayed in the list. Yes
Next review due Date Drives owner reminders. No
Status Choice Draft; Active; Retired. Employee views show Active only. No
Keywords Multiple lines, plain Synonyms employees might search for, e.g. "laptop, computer, monitor". No (searchable)

Category as a Choice column is fine for the pilot. When more departments join, consider a managed metadata term set with Department › Category so each department sees only its own categories.

Views

View Filter, sort, group Used on
All forms Status = Active; sort by Title. All forms page
IT forms Department = Information Technology, Status = Active; group by Category; sort by Title. IT page
Task views (one per task) Task contains value, Status = Active; sort by Title. Homepage task shortcuts
Due for review Next review due within 30 days. Owners
Drafts and retired Status = Draft or Retired. Owners

Formatting

  • Format badge: JSON column formatting on Format renders a blue "Fillable PDF" or green "Online form" pill with an icon.
  • Form cell: JSON formatting on Title shows the form name as the only link, followed by Form ID and Description on a second line.
  • Group headers: view grouping by Category with item counts.

Breadcrumb: modern pages have no automatic breadcrumb. Use static links in the banner Text web part and update them if pages move.

Phones and tablets: sections stack top to bottom in column order: banner, PDF steps, online steps, help, form list. Avoid vertical sections, because their position on phones cannot be controlled.

Search

  • District-wide search uses the standard SharePoint header search. On the Forms Center site it searches the site, so it covers every form, including PDF text and library metadata such as Keywords.
  • IT-only narrowing comes from the IT forms view and column filters. There is no separate in-page search box, because modern pages have no standard search box web part.
  • Promoted results: Microsoft Search editorial bookmarks still work in SharePoint and can surface the most-used forms for common terms. They are created by a search administrator.
  • Later option: if search becomes the main way people find forms, PnP Modern Search (open-source SPFx web parts) adds a page search box with filters by department and format. It is community-supported, installed through the app catalog, and must be kept current by IT. A 2026 performance defect was fixed in version 4.23.3.

Content lifecycle

Stage What happens How
Publish Owner uploads the PDF or adds the link item, fills every required column, and submits it for approval. Content approval; Forms Center owners approve
Update Owner uploads the new version over the old file. Prior versions stay in history. Versioning; Form ID and Title unchanged
Review Owner is reminded before Next review due, confirms or updates the form, and resets both dates. Starting cycle: annual, or sooner when policy changes. Scheduled Power Automate flow
Retire Owner sets Status to Retired. The form leaves employee views but remains in history. Status column; views filter Active

Permissions

  • All employees: read access to the site.
  • Department content owners: edit access to the Forms library; publishing still needs approval.
  • Forms Center owners (District IT): full control; approve content; manage pages and navigation.

A single owners group can edit any department’s forms. If a department needs its forms isolated, use a department folder with unique permissions instead of item-level permissions.

Accessibility

  • Publish only tagged, screen-reader-friendly PDFs, or provide an online form as the alternative.
  • Check text, badges, and banner links for WCAG 2.1 AA contrast. Give the logo alt text and use real headings in Text web parts.
  • DOJ extended the ADA Title II web accessibility compliance dates by one year, to April 2027 for larger public entities. Confirm applicability to WCCCD with counsel.

Dependencies and limitations

  • Header logo: every modern site header has a logo position, and SharePoint shows a generated placeholder if no logo is set. Hiding it needs unsupported CSS. Proposed: Minimal header layout with a small square WCCCD mark as the site logo, with the full logo in the page banner. Confirm with Communications before build.
  • Theme: the WCCCD blue theme must be added by a SharePoint administrator before the banner sections can use it.
  • Reversed logo: a white WCCCD logo file is needed for the blue banner. Request the official asset from Communications.
  • Help Desk channel: confirm the contact method (portal, phone, or email) for the Need help text.
  • Licensing: Microsoft Forms and Power Automate with standard connectors are commonly included in Microsoft 365 plans. Premium connectors, Power Apps beyond standard use, and Power Pages may need added licenses. Confirm against WCCCD’s plans.
